# Framing 3: Decile-Level Null — Proposal's Framing Was the Wrong Question

**Headline thesis:** The proposal's literal question — locate a Z threshold (MW/min) where DOM-zone LMP transitions from linear to heavy-tailed — has been definitively answered "no such threshold exists at any usable resolution," and the right characterization is a smooth, low-discrimination, system-stable Z → LMP relationship; identifying that the proposal's framing was wrong, and replacing it with the correct characterization, is the paper's contribution.

## TL;DR
Three independent lines of evidence (smooth-curve diagnosis from the 7-window TAR sweep, item #6 in-filter direct measurement, item #9 full-panel decile test) triangulate on the same conclusion: Z provides almost no discrimination for "crazy LMP" events. Decile monotonicity fails on 4/4 plotted pnodes; top-vs-bottom Z-decile P(LMP > $100) ratios sit between 1.1× and 1.3×; on dom_zonal the congestion exceedance rate actually *decreases* with Z. The paper's contribution is methodological: a pre-registered test of the proposal's phase-transition framing, a triangulated null, and a refined characterization that replaces "find the threshold" with "Z affects LMP smoothly across the support, with weak discrimination and a small extreme-tail concentration at the top 1%."

## Why this framing is the right one
- **It answers the proposal's actual question, in the proposal's own terms.** Every other framing answers a *different* question (a conditional moment, a mechanism rejection, a post-hoc tail concentration). Only this framing says: "we tested what the grant proposed; the answer is no."
- **Triangulation is unusually strong.** Smooth-curve diagnosis (structural), item #6 (in-filter, n=2027), and item #9 (full-panel, n=31,536) are independent in design but agree in conclusion. Independent confirmation across resolution and filter regime is the gold standard for a null.
- **It is the framing that is hardest to overturn.** Spec A rejection (#1) lives at α=0.05 on one moment of one specification. QR-full (#2) is a single quantile slope. Item #9's decile null is direct, model-free, and reproduces across pnodes — there is no nuisance specification to attack.
- **It reframes every "competing" finding as evidence for itself.** Spec A rejection is *the* evidence that the assumed mechanism is wrong. QR-full positive shows Z matters at moderate quantiles but not at the policy-relevant tail — exactly what "smooth, low-discrimination" predicts. The post-hoc top-1% concentration is the size of the residual signal once the phase-transition model is discarded.

## Supporting evidence
- **Smooth-curve diagnosis (2026-05-13):** 7-window TAR sweep + two 5-min probes show ĉ is filter-, resolution-, and Z-variable-sensitive — the structural signature of a smooth response, not a phase transition. Foundational.
- **Item #9 full-panel decile test (n=31,536):** Per-pnode top-decile P(total_lmp > $250) — primary 0.0190 [0.0146, 0.0247], dom_zonal 0.0206 [0.0158, 0.0260], ashburn_tx1 0.0470 [0.0384, 0.0562]. d10/d1 ratios for P(total_lmp > $100): primary 1.3×, dom_zonal 1.1×, ashburn 1.3×. Congestion d10/d1: dom_zonal 0.7× (decreases with Z). Decile monotonicity fails on 4/4 plotted pnodes. **Verdict C at the decile level.**
- **Item #6 in-filter direct test (n=2027):** P(LMP > $250 | top Z decile) = 0.000 across all 7 pnodes. Within proposal-specified scope, the headline event rate is mechanically zero.
- **Spec A median-split GPD (n=789/half, congestion @ p95):** shape_diff = -0.180, CI [-0.371, -0.044]. Rejects ORDC at α=0.05; direction *opposite* to the proposal. Confirms the assumed mechanism is wrong, not just absent.
- **Post-hoc top-1% Z (n=316):** P(total_lmp > $250) = 4.4% vs. ~1.5% baseline → ~3× lift, not the 10×+ that a phase transition would produce. Consistent with the smooth-low-discrimination characterization, not against it.

## The strongest counter-argument
"A null-result paper is a hard sell for an undergraduate first-author publication. You spent a year of SURG funding to conclude the proposal's question doesn't have a clean answer. The headline is 'we didn't find what we said we'd find.' Where is the contribution? Why is this not just a rejection-rich technical report rather than a paper?"

## Rebuttal
The headline is not "we didn't find it" — it is **"we showed it isn't there, and we replaced it with the right characterization."** Three points.

First, this is a *pre-registered* null on a question with concrete policy implications (data-center load growth and grid management). The phase-transition framing implicitly underwrites every "tipping point" narrative around hyperscale deployment in DOM zone; demonstrating that no such tipping point is detectable in 3.6 years of post-cap PJM data is a substantive claim against a live policy framing. Negative results that overturn a policy-relevant assumption are publishable in energy economics — the discipline has a recent track record (e.g., null findings on demand-response heterogeneity, capacity-market scarcity-pricing efficacy) precisely because the field needs to update its priors.

Second, this is not *only* a null. The triangulation produces a positive characterization: Z → LMP is smooth across the support (TAR), discrimination by Z decile is weak (item #9), in-filter event rates are zero (item #6), and the residual signal concentrates in the top 1% of Z with ~3× lift over baseline (post-hoc). That is a paper-publishable physical-grid characterization of how DOM-zone pricing actually responds to load volatility — replacing a one-parameter threshold model with an empirically-grounded smooth-discrimination model.

Third, the side findings carry real weight as auxiliary contributions: Spec A rejection is a clean methodological result on conditional GPD shape; QR-full positive at moderate quantiles is a useful refinement of the conditional-moment story; the Ashburn TX1 q=0.99 anomaly (LOO-survived) is a documented open question; the 5-min companion's Part B (hourly hides 34-60% of 5-min spikes) is its own data-resolution methodological point. The paper is a chain of pre-registered tests, not a single negative finding.

## Proposed paper title + abstract (~200 words)
**Title:** No Threshold, Smooth Response: A Pre-Registered Test of the Load-Volatility-to-LMP Phase-Transition Hypothesis in PJM's Dominion Zone

**Abstract:** Anticipated data-center load growth in PJM's Dominion zone has motivated a "phase-transition" framing in which sub-hourly load volatility (Z, MW/min) above some threshold drives congestion-price behavior into a heavy-tailed regime. This paper tests that framing directly, using 3.6 years (2022-10 — 2026-05; n=31,536 hourly observations) of Data Miner 2 LMP data at seven pnodes targeting Loudoun County data-center clusters. We pre-register a multi-stage test: a threshold-autoregression sweep across seven (filter × resolution × Z-variable) windows, a median-split conditional GPD on tail shape, a continuous ξ(Z) regression, and a non-parametric Z-decile test of P(LMP exceeds policy-relevant thresholds). The threshold sweep yields no stable ĉ. The median-split GPD rejects the assumed direction (Δξ = −0.18, 95% CI [−0.37, −0.04]). The decile test fails monotonicity on 4/4 plotted pnodes; top-vs-bottom decile event-rate ratios are 1.1×–1.3×. A post-hoc top-1% Z stratum shows ~3× lift over baseline, well below the discontinuity a phase transition would produce. We replace the threshold framing with a smooth-low-discrimination characterization and discuss implications for grid-management triggers and 5-min resolution monitoring.

## What this framing leaves unaddressed
- **JLARC projection (sub-q2)** is not the paper's headline under this framing. It would appear as a discussion section using the post-hoc top-1% lift as the projection coefficient, with explicit acknowledgment that the decile null bounds the projection's policy strength.
- **Mechanism**: the paper documents that the ORDC mechanism (as parameterized by Spec A) is rejected, but does not propose a replacement mechanism. The smooth-low-discrimination characterization is descriptive, not mechanistic.
- **Ashburn TX1 q=0.99 anomaly** is documented as a robust unexplained finding; the paper does not resolve it.
- **Out-of-sample / out-of-zone generalization**: 7 pnodes, 3.6 years, single zone. The null is for DOM at this scale; we do not claim it generalizes to other PJM zones or longer horizons.
- **The advisor's preference for a positive headline**: this framing structurally cannot deliver one without misrepresenting the evidence.

## How this framing handles the competing findings
- **#1 Spec A rejection:** Promoted, not demoted. It is the cleanest piece of evidence that the assumed mechanism is wrong, and it is one of the three triangulated legs of the headline. Sits in §3 (mechanism test) before the decile null in §4.
- **#2 QR-full positive at τ=0.95:** Reframed as "Z affects conditional moderate quantiles but does not transition the tail," appearing as §5 alongside the decile null. Honest framing: real signal, narrower than the proposal asked, consistent with smooth-low-discrimination.
- **#4 Extreme-tail concentration (post-hoc top-1%):** Folded into §4 as the refinement that gives the smooth characterization its quantitative shape — ~3× lift at top 1% Z, vs. the 10×+ a phase transition would imply. Honestly flagged as post-hoc.
- **Item #2 components, item #6 in-filter, item #8 5-min Part B:** All become §6 auxiliary methodological contributions.
