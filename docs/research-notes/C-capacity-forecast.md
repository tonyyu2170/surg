**The uncapped/simulated counterfactual (from the official 2027/2028 BRA Report's Appendix, "BRA Clearing Simulation Without Cap and Floor")** — this is the primary-source basis for the "DOM remains the marginal, most-stressed zone" story: had the FERC-approved price cap/floor not been in effect, the 2027/28 BRA would have cleared with **RTO price $529.80/MW-day and DOM specifically binding above that, at $542.83/MW-day** (a $13.03/MW-day DOM-specific Locational Price Adder) — the *only* LDA that would have bound in that simulation. Total simulated auction value: **$26.3 billion**, i.e. **$9.9 billion higher** than the actual capped outcome. By contrast, PJM's equivalent no-cap/no-floor simulation for the *2026/27* BRA cleared uniformly at **$388.57/MW-day with no DOM-specific adder** — meaning DOM's structural congestion did not bind even in the uncapped counterfactual for 2026/27; it only became the binding constraint in the 2027/28 counterfactual. This is a real, escalating, DOM-specific congestion signal that the price collar has been masking in the *actual* cleared prices for the last two auctions.

**DOM's structural import constraint persists into 2028/29 planning** even though it hasn't produced a separately-priced LDA in the last two actual auctions: the 2028/2029 BRA Planning Period Parameters document states DOM's **CETL/CETO ratio is less than 1**, meaning "an inability to import enough capacity into the DOM ... LDA[s] to satisfy projected reliability needs during certain emergency conditions," and separately notes that **"decreases in the MAAC and SWMAAC LDAs [CETL], and in other eastern LDAs, are all primarily driven by delays to key transmission upgrades, the majority of which are in the DOM LDA."** DOM's CETL was also "slightly lower" than 2027/28. In short: DOM's underlying transmission-import bottleneck is not resolved — it's just that the RTO-wide price cap has, in the last two actual auctions, been reached before DOM's own zonal constraint would separately bind.

Notable operational detail from the 2027/28 BRA report: the **Chanceford-Doubs 500kV backbone transmission line was delayed**, which "significantly impacted MAAC, SWMAAC and DOM Capacity Emergency Transfer Limits (CETLs)" — a transmission delay directly affecting Dominion's import capability, consistent with the CETL commentary above.

### The FERC-approved price cap/floor ("collar") settlement

- **Docket ER25-1357-000**: PJM's proposal for a revised price cap and floor on the Variable Resource Requirement (VRR) curve for the 2026/27 and later BRAs.
- **Origin**: PA Gov. Josh Shapiro filed a complaint against PJM at FERC in Dec 2024 over auction design flaws that threatened large price increases. Shapiro and PJM reached an agreement in January 2025 to lower the cap ~35%; PJM and Shapiro jointly moved to dismiss the complaint Feb 20, 2025.
- **FERC approval**: April 22, 2025, 4-0 vote. FERC: "current market conditions in PJM support PJM's proposal to establish a time-limited 'collar.'" Projected consumer savings: **>$21 billion over the two years** it was originally scoped to cover.
- **Mechanics**: cap of $256.75/MW-ICAP ($333.44/MW-UCAP) and floor of $138.25/MW-ICAP ($179.55/MW-UCAP) applied to the 2026/27 and 2027/28 BRAs; all offers above the cap did not clear, all offers below the floor cleared automatically.
- **Extended via a second docket — ER26-1556-000**: PJM filed Feb 27, 2026 to extend the price cap/floor mechanism to the **2028/29 and 2029/30 delivery years**; FERC accepted the extension **April 28, 2026**. Confirmed directly in PJM's own 2028/2029 BRA Planning Period Parameters document: *"As approved by FERC in Docket ER26-1556, a price cap of $325.00/MW-day (UCAP) and price floor of $175.00/MW-day (UCAP) has been established for the 2028/2029 BRA."* The July 2026 press release calls this "the third consecutive auction with the price collar," negotiated "in coordination with the governors of all 13 PJM states and FERC" — the 2028/29 cap of $325.00/MW-day is a 2.5% step down from 2027/28's $333.44, and the floor also moved from $179.55 to $175.00/MW-day (the report attributes both changes to a shift in the Reference Resource UCAP factor from 77% to 79%).
- **So there are two distinct dockets to cite**: **ER25-1357-000** (original Shapiro settlement, approved April 22, 2025, covering 2026/27 + 2027/28) and **ER26-1556-000** (extension, approved April 28, 2026, covering 2028/29 + 2029/30).
- **Original Shapiro complaint docket** (distinct from the settlement docket): **EL25-46-000**, filed Dec 30, 2024 — this is the underlying complaint; PJM's responsive tariff filing that actually established the collar was docketed separately as ER25-1357-000 pursuant to FPA Section 205 (confirmed via Monitoring Analytics' 2025 State of the Market Report footnote, which cites both dockets explicitly).
- **What the cap saved consumers, quantified from primary sources**: Monitoring Analytics' 2025 SOM Report computes that without the collar (i.e., under PJM's original proposed "unrestricted" VRR curve), the **2026/27 BRA would have totaled $19.29 billion** (vs. actual $16.1B, a $3.17B/19.7% difference) and the **2027/28 BRA would have totaled $26.32 billion** (vs. actual $16.4B, a **$9.91 billion/60.4% difference**) — both figures are independently corroborated by PJM's own no-cap/no-floor simulation in the 2027/28 BRA Report Appendix ($26.3B uncapped total, a $9.9B increase over actual — matching MMU's number almost exactly). This is a more precise, auction-specific version of the "$21 billion over two years" savings figure that circulated at the time of the original 2025 settlement announcement.
- Sources: [RTO Insider — FERC approves cap/floor](https://www.rtoinsider.com/103558-ferc-approves-pjm-pa-agreement-capacity-cap-floor/); [Utility Dive — FERC approves cap/floor](https://www.utilitydive.com/news/ferc-pjm-interconnection-capacity-auction-price-cap-collar/745979/); [PA Governor's office release](https://www.pa.gov/governor/newsroom/2025-press-releases/ferc-approves-gov-shapiro-settlement-pjm-prevent-unnecessary-pri); [Troutman — FERC accepts collar extension](https://www.troutmanenergyreport.com/2026/05/ferc-accepts-pjm-capacity-market-price-collar-extension/); [PJM Board Decision memo on 2028/29+2029/30 collar (PDF)](https://www.pjm.com/-/media/DotCom/about-pjm/who-we-are/public-disclosures/2026/20260212-board-decision-on-price-collar-for-2028-2029-and-2029-2030-capacity-auctions.pdf); primary PJM BRA reports and press releases (fetched in full, see Section 1 table above); [Monitoring Analytics 2025 State of the Market Report for PJM, Vol. 1 (PDF, fetched and grepped in full)](https://www.monitoringanalytics.com/reports/PJM_State_of_the_Market/2025/2025-som-pjm-vol1.pdf).

### 2028/29 auction (held by Aug 2026 — within scope)

Full detail captured above. Key additional facts from the primary PJM press release (July 14, 2026):
- Total procured: 138,318 MW UCAP (RPM) + 10,864 MW UCAP (FRR) = 149,182 MW UCAP.
- Shortfall: 6,831 MW below the one-event-in-10-year reliability standard (up from ~6,500 MW shortfall in 2027/28).
- Reserve margin: 14.7%.
- Resource mix: 46% natural gas, 20% nuclear, 18% coal, 5% DR, 4% hydro, 2% wind, 2% oil, 1% solar. Coal fell 2,941 MW UCAP (retirements/gas conversions); gas rose 5,639 MW UCAP.
- PJM announced it will seek FERC approval for a special **"Backstop Procurement"** in September 2026 to address the near-term shortfall, plus a "Connect and Manage" framework letting large loads (data centers) connect but curtail flexibly during stress events, and a temporary "Expedited Interconnection Track" for up to 10 state-sponsored shovel-ready projects.
- Next BRA (2029/30 delivery year) scheduled for **December 2026** — PJM is working back toward its traditional 3-year-forward planning cycle (auctions were compressed/delayed after the market disruption of recent years).
- Forecast peak load for 2028/29 delivery year is ~2,000 MW higher than the forecast used for 2027/28 (much smaller increment than the ~5,250 MW jump between 2026/27 and 2027/28 forecasts, of which ~5,100 MW was attributed to data centers).

Source: [PJM 2028/2029 BRA press release (PDF, primary, full text captured)](https://www.pjm.com/-/media/DotCom/about-pjm/newsroom/2026-releases/20260714-pjm-capacity-auction-procures-138318-mw-of-generation-resources.pdf).

## 2. PJM's 2026 Long-Term Load Forecast

- **Report**: "2026 PJM Load Forecast Report," posted January 14, 2026. [PDF](https://www.pjm.com/-/media/DotCom/library/reports-notices/load-forecast/2026-load-report.pdf).
- **Headline growth (20-year horizon)**: summer peak demand rises from **160 GW (2025)** to **253 GW by 2046** — a **58% increase**, driven primarily by data centers.
- **Near/medium-term (10-year)**: summer peak load projected to grow **3.6%/year to ~222 GW by 2036**, up from the prior forecast's 3.1%/year trajectory — i.e., the long-run growth rate was revised *upward* even as near-term numbers were trimmed (see below).
- **Data center dominance of growth**: large-load additions (principally data centers) account for **more than 100% of peak demand growth over the next five years**, because "base" (non-data-center) demand is contracting. PJM's large-load adjustments grow **35.1 GW between 2026 and 2031**, against **total** demand growth of only 34.6 GW over the same window (the arithmetic implication is that non-data-center demand is a net *drag*). Over the full horizon to 2046, large loads account for **78% of total growth**.
- **Regional concentration**: **DOM, AEP, ComEd, and PL (PPL)** have the largest data-center pipelines, together accounting for **74% of PJM's total demand growth 2026–2046**. PJM explicitly expects the **Dominion zone (Virginia) to see the largest absolute increase in summer peak demand of any zone from 2026–2030**, driven by data-center load.
- **Methodology change — CONFIRMED from primary source, exact mechanism**: PJM's own report text (fetched and read directly) states: *"The 2026 Long-Term Load Forecast is lower than the 2025 Long-Term Load Forecast in the near term through 2032 due to updates to the electric vehicle forecast, economics and large load adjustments. PJM, in collaboration with stakeholders, created and published a Load Adjustment Request Implementation document to provide transparency in how PJM evaluates large load adjustment requests in the 2026 Long-Term Load Forecast. **Near-term forecast years need "firm" commitments, such as Electric Service Obligation (ESO)/Construction Commitments (CC), while longer-term projects will be considered "non-firm" and will be derated because of their greater uncertainty."*** This is the exact mechanism the task asked about: PJM now requires a **binding contractual commitment (ESO or Construction Commitment) for a data-center request to count at full weight in the near-term forecast**; requests without that firm commitment are classified "non-firm" and **derated** (discounted) rather than counted at face value, with the derating presumably growing more aggressive further out. PJM also published two companion documents for full transparency: a "Load Adjustment Request Implementation" PDF (methodology) and a "Load Adjustment Requests Summary" PDF (the actual request-by-request data) — neither was separately fetched in this research pass but both are linked from the main load forecast report page.
- **Concrete impact of the re-vetting**: comparing the 2026 forecast to the 2025 forecast at three benchmark years, the report shows the summer peak forecast revised **down** by −2,564 MW (2026), −4,414 MW (2028, the year of the RPM auction), and −1,630 MW (2031, RTEP study year) — i.e., a few thousand MW of previously-counted (likely speculative/non-firm) large-load demand was stripped back out specifically around the near-to-medium term.
- **Precise growth figures** (primary source): Summer peak load growth averages **3.6%/year over the next 10 years** and **2.4%/year over 20 years**. Summer peak reaches **222,106 MW in 2036** (a 10-year increase of 65,733 MW) and **253,077 MW in 2046** (a 20-year increase of 96,704 MW). Annualized 10-year zonal growth rates range from **−0.2% to 6.4%**, median 1.6% — meaning DOM (and the other data-center-heavy zones) sit well above the median. Winter peak growth averages 4.0%/yr over 10 years; net energy (GWh) growth averages 5.3%/yr over 10 years, reaching 1,437,629 GWh by 2036.
- **DOM's load adjustment is unique in composition**: PJM's own "Load Adjustments" table lists most zones (AEP, ATSI, APS, BGE, ComEd, Dayton, DLCO, JCPL, METED, PECO, PEPCO, PL) as adjusted solely for "growth in data center load." **DOM is adjusted for "growth in data center load AND a voltage optimization program"** — i.e., DOM's forecast incorporates an additional, DOM-specific grid-modernization factor beyond the data-center driver that appears nowhere else in the footprint.
- Sources (primary, fetched and grepped in full via `curl` + `pdftotext`): [PJM 2026 Load Forecast Report (PDF)](https://www.pjm.com/-/media/DotCom/library/reports-notices/load-forecast/2026-load-report.pdf), posted January 14, 2026. Secondary corroboration: [PJM Inside Lines — 20-year forecast summary](https://insidelines.pjm.com/pjms-updated-20-year-forecast-continues-to-see-significant-long-term-load-growth/); [PowerMag — PJM dials back near-term, keeps long-term trajectory](https://www.powermag.com/pjm-dials-back-near-term-load-outlook-but-maintains-steep-long-term-growth-trajectory/); [Utility Dive — PJM trims near-term forecast on stricter data-center vetting](https://www.utilitydive.com/news/pjm-interconnection-load-forecast-data-centers/809717/); [Modo Energy — data centers define the 2046 forecast](https://modoenergy.com/research/en/pjm-load-forecast-data-centers-2046).

Note: this 300+ page report contains per-zone "dashboard" pages with chart-based summer-peak-by-year visuals (including one for DOM) that did not extract cleanly as numeric text via `pdftotext` (they render as garbled chart-label fragments, e.g. "37% Residential Commercial"). If a follow-up needs the exact DOM-zone summer-peak MW-by-year series specifically, that will require either OCR/manual review of the DOM dashboard page in the PDF, or PJM's companion Excel data tables (the report states "All tables are now provided in Excel format for ease of use").

## 3. Reserve margin / reliability warnings, 2026

### NERC 2026 Summer Reliability Assessment (published ~May 2026)
- PJM shows **adequate reserves for normal/above-normal summer conditions** given strong recent resource additions (record solar/battery/some-gas additions cited nationally).
- **Anticipated reserve margin: 26%** under normal conditions, but this **collapses to 3.8%** under "higher demand, outages, [or] derates in extreme conditions."
- Risk driver: high demand coinciding with low wind/solar output and limited transmission import capability during heat waves, especially where early-summer heat overlaps planned maintenance outages.
- Sources: [NERC 2026 SRA (PDF)](https://www.nerc.com/globalassets/our-work/assessments/nerc_sra_2026.pdf); [Energy Central summary](https://www.energycentral.com/energy-management/post/executive-summary----nerc-2026-summer-reliability-assessment-cl8wkhRf4ANF9ZO).

### NERC Long-Term Reliability Assessment (2025 LTRA, published Jan 2026)
- PJM classified **"Elevated Risk" for 2026–2028**, escalating to **"High Risk" starting in 2029**.
- Anticipated resource margin falls **below the Reference Margin Level (RML) starting in 2029**; NERC projects PJM's reserve margins fall to **about half of RML by 2030**.
- Demand growth in PJM is "growing at its fastest pace in years," driven primarily by data centers, then electrification/manufacturing.
- Caveat flagged in the LTRA itself: newly-approved Reliability Resource Initiative generation projects were **not mature enough to be counted** in the risk analysis — i.e., the "High Risk by 2029" finding could be somewhat conservative/pessimistic if those projects materialize on schedule.
- Sources: [NERC 2025 LTRA (PDF)](https://www.nerc.com/globalassets/our-work/assessments/nerc_ltra_2025.pdf); [PowerMag — NERC warns of mounting risk](https://www.powermag.com/nerc-warns-long-term-grid-reliability-risks-mounting-from-surging-demand-lagging-resources/).

### Generation additions vs. retirements
- **Interconnection queue reform progress**: PJM has processed **>170,000 MW** of new generation requests since 2023; ~**30,000 MW** remained in the "transition queue" to be processed in 2026. The reformed "Cycle" process opened applications with an April 27, 2026 deadline for Cycle 1; **811 new projects (220 GW)** applied in Cycle 1. **~57 GW** of projects have completed study and hold (or were offered) interconnection agreements, free to build — though many remain stalled by non-PJM factors (local opposition, permitting, supply chain, financing).
- **Interconnection Reform Transition Cycle 1** alone is expected to add up to **17.4 GW** (8.4 GW of capacity-accredited UCAP), resource mix 56% solar / 25% wind / 10% storage / 5% hybrid / 3% gas.
- **Retirements**: near-term (through 2026) PJM records **4.2 GW of future deactivations**: 2.2 GW coal, 476 MW gas, 1.5 GW oil/diesel. Broader estimates elsewhere put planned coal retirements at ~1,112 MW (2027) and ~2,680 MW (2028), with operators planning to retire **nearly 20% of current PJM coal capacity by 2028**. In the 2028/29 BRA specifically, coal capacity **fell 2,941 MW UCAP** year-over-year (retirements + gas conversions), while gas **rose 5,639 MW UCAP**.
- PJM has also been **urging delay of announced retirements** (e.g., asked Talen Energy to delay deactivating two units at the 840-MW Herbert A. Wagner plant in MD until transmission upgrades complete, ~2028) and continues **$0 Reliability-Must-Run offers** for specific units to keep them online for reliability while crediting the revenue back to load.
- Sources: [PJM Inside Lines — interconnection reform cycle 1](https://insidelines.pjm.com/pjm-completes-interconnection-reform-transition-cycle-1-studies/); [EPSA — 800+ projects](https://epsa.org/800-projects-enter-pjm-queue-showing-strong-competitive-power-generator-response-to-reliability-needs-energy-prices-and-data-center-growth/); [PowerMag — PJM urges delayed retirement](https://www.powermag.com/pjm-urges-delayed-retirement-of-840-mw-fossil-fuel-power-plant-citing-reliability-impacts/); [Utility Dive — 58 GW at retirement risk](https://www.utilitydive.com/news/pjm-coal-gas-power-plant-risk-retirement-market-monitor/710518/); primary PJM BRA press releases above.

## 4. FERC's 2026 large-load integration actions

Two **distinct** FERC actions are in play — easy to conflate, so distinguished carefully below.

### (A) PJM-specific co-location order — Docket EL25-49-000 (Dec 18, 2025)
- **Origin**: Feb 20, 2025 — FERC opened a Section 206 show-cause proceeding into whether PJM's tariff provisions governing **co-location of generation with load** (data centers/industrial facilities sited behind a generator's meter) are just and reasonable.
- **Order issued**: **December 18, 2025** ("FERC Directs Nation's Largest Grid Operator to Create New Rules to Embrace Innovation and Protect Consumers"), Item E-1, Docket Nos. EL25-49-000 *et al.*
- **Findings**: PJM's tariff is unjust/unreasonable — lacks clarity/consistency on rates/terms for generators serving co-located load and for transmission customers behind those arrangements; existing Behind-the-Meter Generation (BTMG) rules no longer just/reasonable.
- **Directives to PJM**:
  - Revise tariff to specify terms for interconnection customers using generation to serve co-located load.
  - Require the transmission customer serving co-located load to choose among **four transmission service options**: existing NITS; a new Interim Non-Firm service; a new Firm Contract Demand service; or a new Non-Firm Contract Demand service (rates/terms for the two new services to be set via a paper hearing — PJM's initial briefs due **Feb 16, 2026**).
  - Revise BTMG rules, with a transition period and grandfathering for existing contracts.
  - Submit an **informational report by Jan 19, 2026** on reliability concerns tied to co-location, including status of Critical Issue Fast Path proposals (expedited interconnection for shovel-ready generation, reliability-backstop modifications, enhanced load forecasting/demand-flexibility measures).
- A **further order**, also issued June 18, 2026, accepted PJM's compliance filing in part / rejected in part, directed a further compliance filing, and established rates/terms for the new transmission services.
- Source: [FERC Fact Sheet, Docket EL25-49-000 (primary, full text fetched via firecrawl)](https://www.ferc.gov/news-events/news/fact-sheet-ferc-directs-nations-largest-grid-operator-create-new-rules-embrace).

### (B) Six-RTO "large load integration" show-cause orders — the action matching the user's framing ("FERC Launches Aggressive Targeted Action to Speed Large Load Integration")
- **Issued: June 18, 2026.** FERC issued **tailored Section 206 show-cause orders** to **all six** of its jurisdictional RTOs/ISOs: **PJM, MISO, SPP, CAISO, ISO-NE, NYISO**.
- **PJM's specific docket: EL26-67-000** (Item E-7). (The other five: MISO EL26-70-000/E-8; SPP EL26-68-000/E-9; CAISO EL26-71-000/E-10; ISO-NE EL26-72-000/E-11; NYISO EL26-69-000/E-12 — ordering per the FERC fact sheet's item list.)
- **Context**: this action executes on the Secretary of Energy's Advance Notice of Proposed Rulemaking (Docket **RM26-4-000**) — DOE had directed FERC to consider reforms for large-load interconnection; FERC had committed (per an April 2026 notice) to act on that docket by June 2026. Rather than a single nationwide rulemaking, FERC chose **six tailored orders**, citing that the RTOs differ too much in market design/geography/stakeholder structure for one-size-fits-all rules. Staff reviewed **>3,500 pages** of public comment in developing the action.
- **What FERC ordered, mechanically**: each RTO/ISO and its transmission owners have **60 days** (i.e., by **~Aug 17, 2026** for PJM) to either (a) justify why its current tariff remains just and reasonable without large-load-specific provisions, or (b) file tariff revisions addressing FERC's concerns. Separately, within **30 days** (~July 18, 2026), each must file an **informational report** on how it will ensure adequate generation is available to serve existing and new large loads.
- **Five categories of reform** each order tees up:
  1. Efficient transmission service application/study processes (incl. alternative transmission technologies).
  2. Preventing cost-shifting and requiring transparency into transmission cost assignment.
  3. Accommodating co-location agreements and behind-the-meter generation.
  4. New transmission service options for *flexible* large loads.
  5. A process to study generating facilities serving electrically-proximate large/co-located loads.
- **PJM-specific nuance**: PJM's order (EL26-67-000) contains only **four** of the five directive categories — it **omits the standalone "co-location" directive**, because FERC had already addressed PJM co-location separately in EL25-49-000 above. FERC also found PJM's tariff **lacks a definition of "large load" for the PJM Region**; FERC's own working definition used across the six orders is a **peak load >50 MW interconnecting at >69 kV**.
- **PJM's response status (as of Aug 7, 2026 — task date)**: The **60-day tariff-justification/reform deadline is Aug 17, 2026 — not yet due**. The **30-day informational-report deadline (~July 18, 2026) has likely already passed**, but this research did not locate the filed report itself (would require a docket search on PJM's own eTariff/FERC filings for EL26-67-000 — one filing located during research, `20260731-er26-3380-000.pdf`, appears to be a related PJM eTariff filing dated July 31, 2026, but its specific content was not reviewed).

## 5. DOM-zone capacity/energy impact — Monitoring Analytics (Independent Market Monitor for PJM)

**This section was rebuilt from the primary source** — the [2025 State of the Market Report for PJM, Vol. 1 (PDF)](https://www.monitoringanalytics.com/reports/PJM_State_of_the_Market/2025/2025-som-pjm-vol1.pdf), downloaded directly and converted with `pdftotext`/grepped in full (7,578 lines of text; ~66 "data center" mentions, ~16 "Dominion" mentions reviewed). This supersedes and corrects an earlier draft of this section that relied on inconsistent secondary press figures ($6.3B vs $6.5B, two conflicting "40%" claims). The primary numbers below are internally consistent and cross-check cleanly against PJM's own no-cap/no-floor simulation figures in Section 1.

Monitoring Analytics is PJM's Independent Market Monitor (IMM/MMU); its State of the Market reports are the authoritative independent (non-PJM) source on price drivers.

### Exact dollar/percentage attribution to data centers, by BRA (primary, MMU's own methodology: comparing actual auction results to a counterfactual auction run without existing/forecast data-center load in the peak load forecast)

| Delivery Year | $ increase attributable to data-center load | % increase in capacity market revenue attributable to data-center load |
|---|---|---|
| 2025/26 | **≈$9.33 billion** (derived: $16.60B combined 25/26+26/27 total, minus $7.27B for 26/27 alone — MMU did not state the 2025/26-only figure as a standalone headline number in the text reviewed, but it is arithmetically implied by their own combined figures below and matches the "$9.3B" figure that circulated in secondary press) | not separately isolated as a percentage in the text reviewed |
| 2026/27 | **$7,271,197,971** | **82.1%** |
| 2027/28 | **$6,497,653,512** | **65.5%** |
| **Combined 2025/26 + 2026/27** | **$16,603,301,829** | — |
| **Combined 2025/26 + 2026/27 + 2027/28** | **$23,100,955,341** | — |

MMU's exact quote: *"Based on actual auction clearing prices and quantities and uplift MW, inclusion of existing and forecast data center load in the peak load forecast for 2026 resulted in a $7,271,197,971 or an 82.1 percent increase in capacity market revenues for the 2026/2027 RPM Base Residual Auction. ... inclusion of existing and forecast data center load in the peak load forecast for 2027 resulted in a $6,497,653,512, or a 65.5 percent increase in capacity market revenues for the 2027/2028 RPM Base Residual Auction. ... the combined total increase in capacity market revenues resulting from data center load growth ... for the 2025/2026 BRA, the 2026/2027 BRA, and the 2027/2028 BRA of $23,100,955,341."*

MMU's headline framing (verbatim): **"Data center load growth is the primary reason for recent and expected capacity market conditions, including total forecast load growth, the tight supply and demand balance, and high prices."** And, more pointedly: *"The current conditions are not the result of organic load growth. The current conditions in the capacity market are almost entirely the result of large load additions from data centers, both actual historical and forecast."*

### MMU's own counterfactual on the price collar (cross-checks Section 1's DOM figures)
If the 2026/27 BRA had run under PJM's originally-proposed *unrestricted* VRR curve (no collar), total revenues would have been **$19,294,286,100** (+$3,169,915,210 / +19.7% vs. actual). If 2027/28 had run unrestricted, total revenues would have been **$26,324,850,846** (+$9,913,272,621 / +60.4% vs. actual) — this matches PJM's own $26.3B/+$9.9B no-cap simulation almost exactly (see Section 1), and that PJM simulation is the one showing DOM specifically binding at $542.83/MW-day.

### Capacity shortfall trend (MMU's own count, slightly different from PJM's press-release rounding)
*"The amount that PJM is short capacity grew from 208.7 MW in the 2026/2027 BRA to 6,516.6 MW in the 2027/2028 BRA."* (PJM's own press release rounds this to "6,623 MW short" for 2027/28 — a minor MMU-vs-PJM discrepancy, likely a methodological/measurement-basis difference, not corrected further in this research pass.)

### DOM-zone-specific finding: "Data Center Alley" transmission cost-shifting (this is the DOM-isolated finding the earlier draft of this section was missing)
The report has a dedicated case study: *"An area in northern Virginia in the Dominion Transmission Zone, known as Data Center Alley, has experienced significant load growth from data centers. Dominion has presented 44 supplemental project requests to serve the increase in load through the summer of 2025."* PJM's "do no harm" supplemental-planning analysis found a need for **additional baseline transmission reinforcements**, which the **PJM Board approved as $1.4 billion of baseline upgrades specific to the Data Center Alley reinforcements** (2022 RTEP Window 3). Critically, the MMU flags this as a **cost-shifting mechanism**: these costs were allocated under **Schedule 12 of PJM's Open Access Transmission Tariff — i.e., spread across *all* PJM zones by load-ratio share, not charged solely to Dominion or to the data centers driving the need**. MMU's explicit framing: *"The Virginia case illustrates the imposition of transmission costs by data centers on other PJM customers. These additional transmission costs are in addition to the significant capacity market costs imposed on other customers by the actual and forecast addition of large data centers."* This is a concrete, DOM-attributed, dollar-denominated example of the cost-shift concern that motivated FERC's June 2026 large-load show-cause orders (Section 4).

### Energy-market (not just capacity) impact
Total all-in wholesale power cost rose from **$77.78/MWh (Q1 2025) to $136.53/MWh (Q1 2026)** — a **75.5% year-over-year increase** (secondary reporting framed this as a "75-76% surge"). MMU's primary-source framing: *"Data center load growth affects energy market prices. Increased demand puts upward pressure on prices, all else equal. It is difficult to shield customers from the costs caused by data center load growth in the energy market."* Separately, MMU's LMP decomposition for 2025 (RTO-wide, not DOM-specific) found LMP rose $16.99/MWh (+50.4%) year-over-year, with fuel costs explaining 58.5% of that increase and the "transmission constraint penalty factor" component explaining 15.5% — i.e., load growth's energy-price effect flows substantially through fuel-cost and congestion channels rather than being a clean, isolable "data center tax."

### MMU's policy recommendation (context for why this matters going forward)
The MMU is on record opposing continued interconnection of large data-center load without those loads "bringing their own new generation," and has proposed a **"Reliability Backstop Auction"** design — effectively a separate BRA-style auction just for data-center load, matched bilaterally to new generation, so that data-center capacity costs and risks cannot be shifted onto other customers. (Full MMU proposal: [Reliability Backstop Auction Design Proposal – V2 (PDF)](https://www.monitoringanalytics.com/reports/Presentations/2026/IMM_Reliability_Backstop_WS_%20Backstop_Auction_Design_Proposal-V2_20260224.pdf), referenced in the SOM report.) Note PJM's own July 2026 2028/29 BRA press release (Section 1) independently announced it will pursue a "Reliability Backstop Procurement" in September 2026 — language that echoes, though isn't necessarily identical to, the MMU's proposal.
