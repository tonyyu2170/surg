## Coverage / confidence notes

**Well covered by public records:**
- **Winter cold-snap events** (Gerri Jan 2024, the Jan 2025 arctic outbreak, Fern Jan 2026) — PJM's own "Inside Lines" blog and committee presentation decks publish detailed post-event reviews with peak-load MW, forced-outage MW, and alert timelines for every major winter event in the window. High confidence, well-dated. Note the Fern correction above: **always check actual-vs-forecast peak load** — press coverage during an active storm reports the forecast, and the forecast can materially overshoot (as it did for Fern by ~8,300 MW).
- **Summer heat-wave peaks** — same PJM Inside Lines pipeline publishes near-real-time daily peak-load updates during every heat event (2023 through 2026 all now covered, 2023 via the official PJM peak-hour-history table rather than Inside Lines). High confidence.
- **The two major DOM-zone data-center load-loss events** (Jul 10 2024 Fairfax/Ox-Possum, Jul 22 2026 Ashburn) — both generated a NERC-level incident review (2024) or extensive trade-press reconstruction with second-by-second detail (2026). These are the best-documented events in the whole catalog.
- **Capacity market (Base Residual Auction) results** — PJM publishes formal auction reports with exact zonal clearing prices on fixed announcement dates; Monitoring Analytics' quarterly/annual State of the Market reports provide LMP averages. High confidence on dates and price levels.
- **DOE Section 202(c) emergency orders** — DOE publishes the orders themselves as PDFs with effective date ranges. High confidence.
- **PJM-declared emergency alerts/actions (Cold/Hot Weather Alert, Max Gen Alert, Conservative Operations, Load Shed Directive, PAI) for every year 2023–2026 Q1** — Monitoring Analytics' State of the Market reports (Section 3) publish an exhaustive, PJM-audited day-by-day table of every declared alert type with the affected sub-region named (e.g., the Feb 19 2025 action names "DOM_ASHBURN" specifically). This is the single richest structured source found in this research — see Table A above. Getting these PDFs required direct `curl` downloads + local `pdftotext`, since NERC's own PDF host returned HTTP 403 to WebFetch and Monitoring Analytics' PDFs are too large for a single WebFetch pass; the same technique would work for extracting the day-by-day tables from the still-unpulled 2023 Q1–Q4 SOM narrative sections and the 2025/2026 Volume 1 sections not yet parsed here.

**Spotty / low confidence:**
- **2023 was initially miscategorized as a near-total gap in an earlier pass of this research** — that was a research artifact, not a real absence: the first attempt fetched PJM's *markets* year-in-review post instead of the *operations* one, which returned only capacity-performance-penalty commentary. Pulling the Monitoring Analytics 2023 SOM Section 3 tables directly resolved this: 2023 had a Feb 3 RTO-wide Cold Weather Alert, a 9-event Hot Weather Alert series (Jul–Sep) with a confirmed Jul 27 seasonal peak of 146,843 MW, and — notably — **more days of five-minute shortage pricing (46) than any other year in this catalog**, including the two record-setting winter storms. 2023 should not be treated as a quiet baseline year. What's still missing for 2023: DOE OE-417 filings, any transmission/load-loss incident reports, and Monitoring Analytics' hour-level (not just day-level) scarcity-pricing detail.
- **Monitoring Analytics' interval-level (not just day-count) detail on individual scarcity-pricing hours** — Table A gives day-counts per year, which is a good coarse filter, but the SOM reports also contain figures/appendices with the actual 5-minute intervals and $/MWh levels for named events (e.g., Fern's Figure 3-58 shows economic-maximum commitment MW hour-by-hour). Extracting those interval-level series was out of scope for this pass but the source PDFs are now downloaded locally and machine-readable via `pdftotext`, so a follow-up pass can pull them directly rather than re-searching.
- **DOE OE-417 disturbance-report database** was located (ORNL Open Energy Data Portal, doe417.pnnl.gov, EIA's Annual Disturbance Events Archive) but not systematically filtered to Virginia/PJM entries — the dataset is organized as downloadable Excel files per year rather than a searchable web table, and a direct fetch of nerc.com's own incident-review PDF returned HTTP 403 to WebFetch (curl succeeded on other nerc.com-adjacent and monitoringanalytics.com PDFs, so this looks host/UA-specific rather than a hard block — worth retrying with curl + a browser user-agent). The two headline load-loss events (Jul 2024, Jul 2026) are independently well-documented via NERC's public incident review and trade press, but smaller OE-417-reportable disturbances in the DOM zone (fuel-supply emergencies, sub-300 MW losses, etc.) are almost certainly under-represented here.
- **Ordinary distribution-level storm outages** (derecho-type severe thunderstorm events, routine wind/ice damage) are generally NOT well covered by structured public reporting unless they cross a NERC/OE-417 reporting threshold (300+ MW firm load loss, 50,000+ customers for 1+ hour, etc.) — Dominion's own outage-map/press-release history is not a structured historical archive, so only a few large events (Hurricane Helene, one ~200K-customer January 2024 storm) surfaced, both with only approximate dating.
- **Winter Storm naming disambiguation**: search-engine result summarization repeatedly conflated "Winter Storm Fern" across three different years (2023 and 2025 mentions were spurious/hallucinated by the search tool's summarizer; confirmed reliable sourcing places Fern only in **January 2026**, Jan 22–30). The January 2025 event is more reliably attributed to the Blair/Cora/Demi/Enzo storm sequence. Treat any single-source storm-name attribution in older records with caution, and prefer the primary PJM/Monitoring Analytics documents over search-result summaries for exact dates and MW figures — this pass caught and corrected two cases (the Dec 2022 Elliott text bleeding into "2023" searches, and Fern's forecast-vs-actual peak) precisely by going back to primary sources.
- **PJM Performance Assessment Intervals (PAI)**: now confirmed via Table A, not just inferred — **zero PAIs triggered in any year 2023 through Q1 2026.** This is a genuine finding (not a coverage gap): even the two record-cold winter storms and the Aug 11, 2025 BGE load-shed event stayed below the PAI threshold.
- **Time resolution / join granularity**: most rows in the main table carry date-only or date-range precision, appropriate for daily-resolution correlation. A handful carry finer detail worth flagging for a 5-minute-panel join: the Jul 10 2024 and Jul 22 2026 load-loss events have second-level timestamps (times as-reported in EDT, not normalized to any other convention); the Jun 23 2025 and Jan 25–26 2026 price spikes have hour-level timestamps; Table A's "Shortage Pricing" day-counts are aggregates of five-minute intervals whose exact times are in the source PDFs' figures but were not extracted here. Do not assume any non-timestamped row applies to the full 24 hours of its listed date.

**Row count:** 31 dated/dateable rows in the main table (28 within window, 2 explicitly marked outside-window, 1 full-year 2023 baseline row), plus the separate 7-row Table A annual census (2023–2026 Q1) that is itself sourced from four independent Monitoring Analytics reports.
